# Praedra (playable prototype)

A rough but playable front end for the rules core in `core/`. Pick a side, press Play, give orders to fleets and build on your planets. All rules live in `core/`; this folder only draws the screen, submits orders and calls the day step. The computer plays the other side (the core's simple AI). New games use the core's 360-system start.

## Launch it on the Mac (Apple Silicon or Intel)

1. Download `Praedra-mac.zip` and double-click it to unzip. You get **Praedra.app**. Drag it to Applications (or leave it in Downloads).
2. The app is not signed by an Apple developer account, so macOS blocks it the first time. Pick one way:
   - **Terminal:** open Terminal and paste (change the path if the app is elsewhere):
     ```
     xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine ~/Downloads/Praedra.app
     ```
     then double-click the app.
   - **No Terminal:** double-click the app once, close the warning, open **System Settings > Privacy & Security**, scroll down and click **Open Anyway**, then confirm.
3. Linux / Steam Deck: unzip `Praedra-linux.zip`, run `linux/Praedra.x86_64` (keep the `.pck` and the `data_...` folder next to it).

The first time you press **New Game**, a **How to play** page opens. You can open it again any time from the menu (top left, **How to play**) or with **F1**.

## How to play (short)

- **The screen** (Paul's mockup, 2026-10-28; top bar from his Paper frame "ui-update-nov-15", 2026-11-15). The map fills the window. Across the top a dark strip with boxes hanging from it, in pixel words (BitPotion): the menu (≡) and **Goal**, the search and **Layers** (the search results and the layers list hang under their boxes, in the same pixel words: grey, lighter under the mouse, light for what is on), then Raw, Refined and Upkeep with their pixel icons, the day, one Play / Pause button and the speeds. Under the bar, centred, a pill that says why the clock stopped or how many things need you (click it for Needs you); the key of a map layer sits at the top left. With a larger interface size the search narrows first, then the stocks keep only their icons. Bottom left: the map legend, when you ask for it (Layers > Legend). Bottom centre: a card for what you clicked (owner, support, troops, fleets; for a fleet where it is and goes) with **Build** or **Move...** and **Details →**. Bottom right: the one place for news, short one-line notes that fade (red news, real good news and the answers to your own orders; click one that names a place to go there); the **Log** (E) has every message, by kind. **H** centres the map on your HQ, **Shift+H** on the enemy's once you have found it, and both are in the menu (**Go to your HQ**, **Go to enemy HQ**). Blue is always you, orange the enemy, grey nobody, a hollow ring a system you never explored.
- **Goal.** The Hand wins by taking the Hearth (the Collective headquarters) **and** holding its two key leaders prisoner; the Collective wins by taking Charter and holding the Hand's two. A key leader who dies no longer counts. Either side also wins when the other holds no system, or by holding 70% of the systems the two sides hold for 240 days after the enemy capital fell. The **Goal** button says how close you are, and turns into a countdown ("You win in N days", red "Enemy wins in N days") once that race runs. The Hand does not know where the Hearth is at the start: the menu's **Go to enemy HQ** is greyed and says so.
- **The window.** **Details →**, the menu or a key opens a window on the right with tabs: **Needs you** (Q), **Selected**, **Fleets** (F), **Leaders** (C), **Missions** (M) and **Log** (E). Its cross or Esc closes it. While it is open a map click shows the clicked thing in it; while it is closed a map click only fills the card. **Needs you** lists what wants a decision, worst first, each with the button that fixes it. **Run until something needs me** plays until a new problem appears.
- **Search** (/): type part of a name to find a system, one of your fleets or one of your leaders. Enter picks the first hit. While you move a fleet, picking a system sends the fleet there.
- **Map layers** (the **Layers** switch opens the list at the top left; V cycles them): Owners, Support, Trouble, Yards (yellow = idle), Intel age (bright = seen now) and Slots, then **Routes** (L) and **Legend**. A small key for the shown layer stays at the top left, list open or closed. **Names** (map declutter, 2026-11-18): the map opens with dots and glyphs only. Your own systems are named from a closer zoom, with the short names of your fleets and of everything of yours that moves ("Fleet 1", "The Chair", "Espionage"). Any other system is named only while you point at its sector or one of its dots, or select it. Point at a fleet or a mover, or select a fleet, to read "Fleet 1 · day 6". Hold **Alt** (Option) to see every name and every label at once.
- **Sectors on the map.** Each sector (the group of about ten systems) is a faint tile with a gap around it. Point at the open space of a sector: the tile lights up and its name and its systems' names show. Click it to open the sector window. Rest the mouse on a dot for a one-line card about that system.
- **The sector window** (click a sector, or S for the sector of the selected system): every system of the sector as a card with its planet (drawn by the Planet Maker), who holds it, support, energy (building slots: free of total), raw slots, what its mines and refineries make in 30 days, troops, and what its yards build. The head sums the sector and shows a small map of it. Each card has three links, **Buildings**, **Fleets** and **Forces**, that open the system's own window over a column of cards: build, scrap, command a fleet in orbit, train troops, send a mission. Left and Right arrows step through the sectors; Esc closes the system window, then the sector window. Systems you don't hold show what your side last saw, with the day.
- **Fog of war.** You see live only the systems you hold or have a fleet at. Others show what your side saw last, with the day; the card turns yellow when the picture is more than 10 days old. Enemy fleets are drawn only where you see them. The computer opponent plays by the same rule.
- **Time.** Play (▶ or Space) runs the clock; the same button, now ||, stops it (or Space). The buttons 1x, 2x, 5x set the speed (keys 1-3): a day lasts 8 seconds at 1x, 4 at 2x, 1.6 at 5x (10x went with the new top bar, 2026-11-15). **N** moves one day. "Run until something needs me" skips quiet days at 10 a second. The clock stops by itself after a battle, a capture or an invasion; **Menu, Settings** adds more stops. Orders start when the next day starts; a note under the top bar counts the ones waiting, and they show on the map as white dashed lines.
- **Move a fleet.** Select it (map, Fleets tab or search), press **Move...**, click the destination. The order bar shows the route and the arrival day. Shift-click adds a waypoint, Backspace drops the last one. A route through a known enemy trap turns red: **Go anyway**, **Go around via X**, or Esc to cancel. The game never reroutes you by itself.
- **Fleets.** The Selected tab shows the ships by class, fighters, regiments aboard, troop and fighter room, officers, and **Split, transfer or merge**. **Load troops** keeps the planet's garrison safe.
- **A fleet's window** (**F** opens the selected fleet's, or the panel's fleet card; Paul 2026-11-21: one fleet, done well, one column so four stand side by side; then his Paper design, fleet-new-frame-1 and -2, in the Infrastructure window's look). The head: the name, ⋮⋮ – ×, and the **status** ("In orbit at Tiryal", or "Moving to Tiryal / ETA: 29 days" over a 2 px bar of the trip; its hover gives the fleet's strength and slowest ship), then the orders it has, each with Cancel. A strip of tabs: **Command** (Fleet actions: **Bombardment ▾** (military, civilian or everything), **Invasion**, **Move**, the harvester's **Destroy System**; Fleet behavior: **Defensive** or **Elusive**, this fleet's own (a fleet split off copies it; in a battle with both, the Elusive fleets pull back at once and the rest fight); Fleet command: the Admiral, General and Commander as cards; Passengers; On the way), **Ships** (one row per ship: its class, its fighters and regiments out of their room, its hull; its damage in words in its hover), **Fighters** and **Troops** (cards, their counts out of the room on the tab). Click a ship to pick it, Shift+click several: **Split off** and **Scrap** show over the rows; right-click a ship to split it off or scrap it; while a ship is dragged a dashed place under the rows takes it as a new fleet. **Right-click the head** to merge another fleet into this one (those here first, joining at once, then the others closest first, flying here to join; a known trap on the way is named) or to scrap the whole fleet; or drag a fleet's ⋮⋮ onto another fleet's window. While **Move** waits for a destination the windows step aside (Esc cancels and brings them back). **Several fleets** are picked on the map: **Shift+click** their markers (the selected fleet counts as the first), then **right-click** a system to send them all (move only; Esc lets them go); **Shift+right-click** starts the move with that system as a waypoint. Each fleet flies its own path and each path is checked: if any crosses a known trap the order bar waits, with **Go anyway** and **Send only the fleets with no trap**.
- **Invade.** Load regiments, fly to the enemy or neutral system (enemy fleets there fight you first), optionally **Bombard** (military, civilian or everything), then **Invade**, both in the fleet's window. One attack order per fleet per day.
- **Build.** Select one of your systems, open **Build**, pick Ships, Facilities, Troops or Fighters, set how many, click a row. Each row shows cost, upkeep and days, and says why it cannot be built now. **Stop...** on a busy yard asks first and says what is lost. Idle yards show in Needs you, on the Yards layer and in the sector windows.
- **Money.** **Raw** is ore from mines; refineries turn it into **Refined**, which you spend. **Upkeep** (used / limit) is what everything costs; over the limit, units get scrapped, and Needs you warns before that.
- **Leaders** (C): your leaders with where they are and their post, what the enemy needs from you, enemy leaders you have seen (key ones marked "you must capture"), and the fallen. Give a leader a fleet post (Admiral, Commander, General) or a system General post, move one alone, or send a team on a mission.
- **Missions** (M): each mission's team, target and phase, with **Cancel**. Results come in the Log.
- **The Hearth (Collective).** Select it (**H**), press **Move HQ...**, click one of your systems.
- **Save / Load.** **Menu, Save game...** has six slots with the day and side; overwriting asks first. **Ctrl+S** (Cmd+S) saves to slot 1. The game autosaves every 30 days; **Continue** on the start screen loads that. Load and Back to the start screen ask before they replace the running game.

## For developers

`Scripts/GameBoot.cs` is the one adapter to the core's setup (new game from `NewGame.Create`, the simple AI as opponent with `AiUsesFog` = true, the victory check).

Fog of war: the screens read other sides only through the core's fog API for the player's side (`host.Fog`: `Picture`, `IsExplored`, `Sees`, `VisibleFleets`, `KnownHq`, `EventsFor`); `GameSession` wraps these (`KnownOwnerOf`, `LastSeenDay`, `MyHqSystem`, `EnemyHqSystem`). Own fleets, systems and the Collective's own HQ are read from the game as before (they are the side's own).

Characters: the screens read the player's own characters from `host.Characters`; enemy leaders only through the fog (`Fog.CharactersSeen`: where and when your side saw them) plus the key leaders the win terms name; character messages come from the fog-filtered feed (`EventsFor`). The five orders (`AttachCharacter`, `DetachCharacter`, `AssignSystemGeneral`, `ReleaseSystemGeneral`, `MoveCharacter`) are submitted with `Game.Submit`; refusals are worded by `GameSession.WhyCharacter`.

Missions: the screens list only the player's own mission groups (`host.Missions.Missions`, own state) and special forces. Which kinds a team can do, whether a target is allowed, the route and the arrival day all come from the core's read-only dry run `MissionModule.CheckStart(StartMissionOrder)` (the same checks as the order handler; it changes nothing and draws nothing). The orders `StartMissionOrder` and `CancelMissionOrder` are submitted with `Game.Submit`; refusals are worded by `GameSession.WhyMission` (every `MissionReject` code). Mission messages come from the fog-filtered feed (`EventsFor`): the mission side's own events, and `AgentDetected` for the defender.

Files: `Main.cs` (screens, command-line modes, headless smoke test), `StartMenu.cs`, `GameBoot.cs` (the adapter to the core's setup and saves), `GameSession.cs` (queued orders tracked, core events turned into plain messages, fog-filtered reads). The game screen is one `GameScreen` class split by area: `GameScreen.cs` (loop, keys, Esc), `.Shell.cs` (the floating pieces: menu, layers, search, pill, clock, stocks, toasts, legend), `.Hud.cs` (the selection card and the message corner), `.Drawer.cs` (the window and its tabs), `.Attention.cs` (Needs you), `.System.cs` (Selected: a system), `.Fleet.cs` (Selected: a fleet, split and merge, attacks), `.Build.cs` (build table, queues, Stop, where research stands), `.Scrap.cs` (scrapping ships, fighters, regiments and buildings), `.Move.cs` (move mode, order bar, traps, waypoints), `.Map.cs` (map layers, sector rows), `.Search.cs`, `.Goal.cs`, `.Log.cs` (log, toasts, unread count), `.Dialogs.cs` (menu, saves, settings, How to play, victory), `.Characters.cs` (Leaders tab), `.Missions.cs` (Missions tab and planner), `.Playtest.cs` (click helpers for the playtest and screenshot options). `Ui/UiKit.cs` is the look (colours, fonts, theme, cards, procedural icons; the fonts are Inter, JetBrains Mono and Space Grotesk, all SIL OFL, and BitPotion for window names and the sector tiles' system names, CC BY 4.0, which the credits must name; licences in `fonts/`). The windows (Ground, Fleet, sector, and the Infrastructure window of a planet you don't hold) follow Paul's "styles" guide (2026-11-14): `Ui.WindowTheme` plus the `using (Ui.Window())` scope give them Space Grotesk, the greys of Paul's Infrastructure design (2026-11-19: #141416 surface, #BABABA words, #676767 under the mouse, #454545 greyed out, #2D2E32 lines, #909EBD window buttons; `Ui/Look.cs`), no fills and square corners; colour only where it means something. Your own planet's Infrastructure window (`GameScreen.WinBuild.cs`) is Paul's Paper design of 2026-11-18, in the colours of `Ui/Look.cs`: the name, the energy and raw material gauges (bright built, mid ordered, dark free), Ground and military and Fleet; a strip of cells that are the Build tabs (Construction yards, Orbital shipyards, Training facilities; the arrows show Mines, Refineries and Defences); under it the open cell's buildings (right-click to scrap) and what can be built as cards (refined and days; click to order How many). A yard cell says "Building:" and what on hover; right-click it to stop its work or cancel an order. The other windows take these colours next. `Ui/SystemTile.cs` the sector window's system tile (Paul's Paper frame "system-info"), `Ui/UiSettings.cs` the settings file, `Ui/SectorWindow.cs` the sector table. `GalaxyView.cs` draws the map, `FleetLayer.cs` the fleets, `GalaxyBackdrop.cs` + `GalaxyBackdrop.gdshader` the galaxy picture behind it (procedural, drawn per pixel into a buffer that is redrawn only when the view moves; its arms follow the spiral the systems were laid out on and its light follows where the systems are). Playtests: `Main.Playtest.cs` and `Main.Playtest*.cs` (characters, missions, harvester, routes, screens).

Needs the **Godot 4.7.1 .NET (mono)** editor and the **.NET 8 SDK**. The project is NOT in `Praedra.sln` (CI has no Godot SDK). Data: the csproj copies `data/design/**.csv` and `data/classic/*.csv` to `godot/data/**.txt` (git-ignored) before every build, because Godot drops `.csv` from exports; at start the game copies them to the user folder as `.csv` for the core's `GameData.Load`.

```
cd godot
dotnet build PraedraViewer.csproj -p:RestoreAdditionalProjectSources=/tmp/godot/nupkgs   # or the nupkgs folder of the editor download
godot --headless --path . -- --smoke      # 60 days of a new game with scripted orders, plus a save/load round trip
godot --headless --path . -- --playtest   # plays Collective and Hand games through the real screens (see below); exit code 1 on failure
```

Command-line options after `--` for screenshots and quick looks: `--screenshot out.png [frames]`, `--menu`, `--newgame`, `--side Collective|Hand`, `--preset easy|medium|hard`, `--days N`, `--rules new|classic` (space battle rules, DEFAULT new), `--focus "System" [zoom]`, `--fleet "Name"`, `--moveto "System"` (needs `--fleet`), `--howto`, `--filter All|Battles|"My systems"`, `--zoom Z`, `--characters` (open the Leaders tab), `--click "Button text"` (click a panel button by the start of its text), `--pick "System"` (answer a destination click, e.g. after `--click "Move Nobody..."`), `--hover "System"` (hover a system: the route preview of a move or mission target), `--missions` (open the Missions list), `--open Build|Log|Selected|...` (a drawer tab or a Selected sub-tab), `--layer Support|Trouble|Yards|Intel|Slots`, `--sectors` (open the sector windows), `--sectorhover Sector_Name` (light a sector as if the mouse pointed at it), `--mapclick "System"` (select as a map click: the card, no window), `--close` (close the window), `--gamemenu` (open the menu), `--goal` (open the Goal window), `--dialog "How you win"|Load|Save|Settings|"How to play"|Victory|Report` (open a dialog; with `--uiscale 1.4` it shows how it fits the short screen of the largest text size), `--trap "System"` (a move whose route crosses a trap), `--preview "System"` (a move preview), `--window ground|troops|fighters|fleets|lore|infra System` and `--window fleet System:Tab` (a management window), `--sector Name_Sector`, `--strike "System"` (a harvester of yours destroys it, for a harvested system), `--dump-ui out.json` (with `--screenshot`: what every control draws plus a map-only picture, for tools/paper-sync/). Screenshot with the software renderer: `xvfb-run -a -s "-screen 0 1440x810x24" godot --path . --rendering-driver opengl3 --resolution 1440x810 -- --screenshot map.png 30 --newgame`.

Export (templates 4.7.1 mono installed): `godot --headless --path . --export-release macOS ../build/mac/Praedra-mac.zip` and `--export-release Linux ../build/linux/Praedra.x86_64`. Builds go under `build/` (git-ignored); never commit them.

**Mac signing.** Godot's own ad-hoc signing from Linux makes an app macOS kills at launch on Apple Silicon ("zsh: killed", seen 2026-10-13), so the macOS preset has `codesign/codesign=0` and **every Mac build must come from `godot/build-mac.sh`** (a plain Godot export is unsigned and will not start on Apple Silicon). The script signs after export with rcodesign (apple-codesign 0.29.0, Paul approved 2026-10-13): unzip the export, `rcodesign sign --entitlements-xml-file mac-entitlements.plist Praedra.app`, then re-zip with `zip -qry` (keeps permissions). Ad-hoc, no hardened runtime; the entitlements let .NET run its JIT. macOS still asks for **Open Anyway** once per download (no notarization without a paid Apple account).
